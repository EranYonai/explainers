# Shared brief: explainer prototypes

You are building one prototype explainer for Eran's project: a teaching site plus an "explainer engine".
Read the plan page first (open it in a text editor; it contains the style
tokens, the working git-bisect step demo with its JS, the AI-first authoring flow, and the "does the YAML
idea hold up" stress test). Your explainer is a test of that plan, so your notes matter as much as the page.

## Who it's for
Working engineers who want to understand a concept properly. Smart, busy, reading on a phone as often as
a laptop. Eran teaches in text, animation and drawing. No voice, no video. The page carries the teaching.

## The format ideas being tested
- A scene is a set of objects plus numbered steps. Each step says what is TRUE (states), and the engine
  animates the change between steps. "Scenes describe states, not animations."
- Three layers: **states** (hand/AI-written YAML), **traces** (steps generated by running real code),
  **components** (a JS escape hatch with typed params for continuous/random/data-heavy things, wired to
  sliders from YAML).
- One source should later produce: a scroll page, a step-through embed, silent MP4 clips.

## Deliverables (write all three into your assigned folder)
1. `index.html`: the finished explainer. One self-contained file.
2. `scene.yaml`: the scene(s) of your explainer written in the proposed format, as if a compiler existed.
   Invent the syntax you need, but keep it consistent with the bisect example in the plan
   (`scene`, `objects`, `steps` with `say` + `do`). Use `trace:` and `component:` blocks where the
   states layer isn't enough, and mark anything you had to invent with a `# NEW:` comment.
3. `NOTES.md`: an honest field report, under ~400 words:
   - Which parts fit the states layer, which needed traces or components, and why.
   - Primitives you used or wished existed.
   - What the YAML could NOT express that you did in HTML/JS by hand.
   - What a compiler would have saved you, and where it would have got in the way.
   - What an AI author would likely get wrong here (for the self-check step).
   - One-line verdict on the plan.

## Page requirements
- Self-contained HTML. No external requests except Google Fonts. No libraries needed; if you want a
  hand-drawn look, use an SVG filter (feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap), not a library.
- Reuse the plan's look: same color tokens (paper, surface, ink, muted, line, accent, hl, good, bad),
  fonts (Bricolage Grotesque display, IBM Plex Sans body, JetBrains Mono code), light AND dark theme
  (bare :root, prefers-color-scheme dark guarded by :root:not([data-theme="light"]), and
  :root[data-theme="dark"]). Explicit body background.
- Mobile first. Must look good at 390px wide with no sideways page scroll. SVG text must stay readable
  on a phone (scale labels up or change the viewBox at narrow widths, as the plan's demo does).
- Each scene: step-through controls (Back / Next / Play), a caption with aria-live, keyboard arrows,
  respects prefers-reduced-motion. Complete at rest: the first frame is meaningful before any click.
- Prose around the scenes: roughly 400-800 words total. Short, direct sentences. Accurate, technically
  correct. No em-dash asides, no emoji, no "not X but Y" constructions.
- Give the page a real <title>.

## Check once, then finish
Python Playwright is installed and Chrome is at /usr/bin/google-chrome. Render your page once at
390x844 and once at 1100x900, step a scene forward a few times, check `document.documentElement.scrollWidth`
equals the viewport width at 390, and look at the screenshots. Fix what you see, one pass. Do not publish
anything anywhere. Finish with a 5-line summary: folder path, what the explainer covers, scene count,
and your one-line verdict.
